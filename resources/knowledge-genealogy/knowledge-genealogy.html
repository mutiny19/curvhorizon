Five traditions
On accuracy — why a clean tree oversimplifies

Medicine predates biology. As a healing art it is ancient; only modern scientific medicine is biologically grounded. The chain shown, Medicine → Pathology → Oncology, is the real specialization path.

Psychology and neuroscience are dual-rooted. They grew from experimental physiology as much as from philosophy of mind. They sit in the Mind tradition here for narrative clarity, with a glowing link back to physiology on hover.

Economics came from moral philosophy. Adam Smith held a chair in moral philosophy, not economics; the field began as "political economy."

Mathematics is only partly a descendant. It has ancient, semi-independent roots, but its foundations are a philosophical project (Frege, Russell, Gödel), which is why it hangs under logic and epistemology.

Interdisciplinary fields have no single parent. A strict tree cannot show this, so cognitive science, biochemistry, bioinformatics, computational linguistics, behavioral economics and others carry a dashed edge and reveal their second parent as a link across the core when hovered.
